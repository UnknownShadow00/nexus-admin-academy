## 1. What is this?

The motherboard is the main circuit board. Its form factor defines its physical
size, mounting points, and broad case/power layout. The specific board—not the
form factor alone—determines CPU socket/chipset, RAM type, connectors, and
expansion capacity. ATX is larger; microATX is smaller; mini-ITX is compact.

## 2. Why does an IT worker care?

A part can be electrically capable but physically incompatible. Support staff
must check the system or board manual before ordering or opening equipment.

## 4. What you need to remember

- Match form factor, CPU socket/chipset, RAM generation, case, and PSU.
- Smaller boards may fit larger cases; larger boards do not fit smaller cases.
- Record cable locations and use ESD controls before removal.
- A firmware update is not a routine first step; use it only for a documented need.

## 5. Real workplace example

A replacement microATX board fits an ATX case, but the old CPU is unsupported
by its socket. Checking both dimensions before purchase prevents a failed visit.

## 6. Commands / tools

Use the system model, vendor service manual, an ESD strap/mat, labels, and a
parts inventory. Windows `msinfo32` can help identify the current platform.

## 7. Interview / example

**How do you decide whether a motherboard replacement is compatible?** Check
case/form factor, socket and chipset support, RAM, power connectors, expansion,
front-panel connections, and the vendor documentation before ordering.
