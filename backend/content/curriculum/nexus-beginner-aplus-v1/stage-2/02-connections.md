## The outside of the computer

A **peripheral** is a device connected to a computer, such as a mouse, keyboard, monitor, or printer. A **port** is a socket where a cable or accessory connects. The plug and port must match; never force a connector.

A common rectangular **Universal Serial Bus (USB)** port can connect a keyboard or mouse. A wide **High-Definition Multimedia Interface (HDMI)** port often carries a picture to a monitor. Ports can look similar at first, so look at the shape and the label before touching a cable. You will learn more connector types later.

Open the original port picture and identify the labeled USB port in the interaction. The picture is a simplified teaching diagram, not a photograph or a complete hardware guide.

## Choose a safe first check

If a monitor looks blank, ask whether it is powered on and whether the correct input is selected. If checking a cable, make sure it is safe to reach and ask before disconnecting it. Stop if you see damage, smell burning, or need to open equipment. Never open a power supply; ask a trained technician.

## Why this matters at work

A loose or wrong connection is common. A careful visual check can save time without risking equipment or data.
