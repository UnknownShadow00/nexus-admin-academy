## Three different things

A **file** is saved information, such as a photo or a document. A **folder** groups files so they are easier to find. An **application** is software you open to do a task, such as writing a document. The application can open or save files, but the file is not the application.

Picture a paper office: a document is like a file; a labeled drawer is like a folder; the tool you use to write is like an application. This comparison is imperfect, but it helps you ask the right question: “Is the document missing, or is the application failing to open it?”

Open the file-and-application picture. Then type the simple term requested in the interaction. The checkpoint checks whether you can tell these things apart and choose a sensible first question.

## Why this matters at work

Someone saying “my Word is gone” might mean the application is missing, or they might mean they cannot find a document. Ask before changing or deleting anything.
