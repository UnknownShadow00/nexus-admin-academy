## A simple troubleshooting habit

**Troubleshooting** means finding the cause of a problem in a careful order. First, ask what the person expected and what actually happened. Next, check one safe, simple possibility. Then test whether the problem changed. If it did not, ask for help or try another approved check. Do not make random changes.

**Verify** means check the result yourself or with the user. If you did not verify a fix, say so. Never write “fixed” or “completed” just because you tried something. A truthful update might be: “I restarted the application, but the user has not yet confirmed it works.”

## When to stop

Pause and ask a supervisor or experienced technician when you lack permission, when data might be lost, when equipment looks damaged, or when the next step could affect other people. This is called **escalating**: passing the problem to someone with the right access or skill. It is a responsible action, not a failure.

Open the decision picture, then choose the safe next action in the interaction. The checkpoint includes a situation where the correct answer is to report that work has not been verified.

## Why this matters at work

People depend on accurate updates. Honesty and safe checks protect their work.
