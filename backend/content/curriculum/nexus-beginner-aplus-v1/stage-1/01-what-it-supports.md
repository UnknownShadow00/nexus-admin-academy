## Start here

**Information technology (IT)** is the people and tools that help an organization use computers and information. Think of a worker trying to sign in, open an application, or print a document. IT helps the worker do that safely and reliably.

IT may support a **device** (a laptop or printer), an **application** (a program such as a calendar), an **account** (the worker's way to sign in), or a **network** (the connection that lets devices share information). A **user** is the person trying to get work done. These are different places to look when something goes wrong. You do not need to fix a network yet; just recognize that it is one possible problem area.

## Two useful labels

**Hardware** means physical parts you can touch, such as a keyboard or screen. **Software** means instructions a device runs, such as a web browser. A **browser** is an application used to view websites. A broken screen and a browser that will not open are different problems.

## Why this matters at work

If someone says “my computer is broken,” a support technician asks what they were trying to do and what happened. That short question often tells you whether to start with the device, application, account, or connection.
