# Lesson 5 — Battery, Charging, Screen & Physical Damage

## 1. What is this?
Objective 5.4 moves from “what hardware exists” to **what symptoms mean and what you should do next**. Common physical/power symptoms include:
- poor battery health
- swollen battery
- broken screen
- improper charging
- liquid damage
- overheating
- digitizer problems
- physically damaged ports
- cursor drift/touch calibration

## 2. Why does an IT worker care?
The wrong troubleshooting action can make a mobile problem worse. A damaged lithium-ion battery or wet device is not a normal “keep testing until it works” situation.

The support goal is:
**collect evidence → protect people/data/device → perform only safe approved checks → repair, replace, or escalate → verify.**

## 4. What you actually need to remember
**Poor battery health**
- reduced runtime does not automatically mean charging hardware is bad
- inspect health/capacity information where available
- replace only with approved compatible service procedure

**Swollen battery**
- discontinue normal use/charging
- do not puncture, bend, compress, or force the enclosure closed
- follow vendor/company safety and trained-service/disposal procedure

**Improper charging**
- inspect cable/charger compatibility and condition
- test with known-good approved accessories
- inspect port for debris/looseness/damage without forcing it
- “works only when held at an angle” strongly suggests a physical connection issue

**Liquid damage**
- stop normal operation/charging and follow the vendor/org procedure
- do not use heat or improvised methods

**Screen vs digitizer**
- display layer creates the image
- digitizer/touch layer detects touch
- good image + dead touch region points toward digitizer/touch input
- offset touches may indicate cursor drift/calibration or hardware trouble

**Overheating**
- reduce workload/charging as appropriate
- note when/where it happens
- investigate battery, charger, apps/workload, environment, and hardware

## 5. At work
A user's phone charges only when the USB-C cable is held upward. A known-good cable produces the same symptom. Instead of forcing the connector or repeatedly bending it, the technician documents the evidence and inspects/escalates the port as a likely physical fault.

## 6. Commands / Tools
- battery health/status
- known-good approved charger/cable
- visual inspection
- touch-screen diagnostic/calibration if the model supports it
- vendor service diagnostics
- asset/warranty record
- documented safety/escalation procedure

## 7. Interview / Explain
**Prompt:** “What would you do if a user hands you a phone with a swollen battery?”

A strong answer prioritizes safety: discontinue use/charging, do not puncture/compress it, isolate it according to workplace procedure, and route it to trained service/disposal.
