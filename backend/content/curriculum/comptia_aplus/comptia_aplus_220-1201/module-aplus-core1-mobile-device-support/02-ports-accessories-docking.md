# Lesson 2 — Ports, Accessories & Docking

## 1. What is this?
Mobile devices connect to power, data, networks, and accessories in several ways. A+ expects you to recognize **USB/USB-C, microUSB, miniUSB, Lightning, NFC, Bluetooth, tethering/hotspots**, plus accessories such as styluses, headsets, speakers, webcams, docking stations, port replicators, trackpads/drawing pads, and track points.

The job skill is not memorizing pictures. It is choosing the **right connection or accessory for the user's need**.

## 2. Why does an IT worker care?
A large percentage of “device problems” are actually:
- the wrong cable or adapter
- an incompatible stylus/accessory
- a damaged port
- a dock that is not connected or powered as expected
- a peripheral that needs Bluetooth pairing
- a user expecting one connector to support a feature the device does not support

## 4. What you actually need to remember
- **USB-C:** reversible; commonly supports power and data. Some devices also support display or other alternate modes, but do not assume every USB-C port/cable has identical capabilities.
- **microUSB / miniUSB:** older, smaller USB connector types still seen on legacy devices/accessories.
- **Lightning:** Apple proprietary connector found on many earlier iPhones/iPads.
- **Bluetooth:** short-range wireless peripherals such as headsets, speakers, keyboards, and many styluses.
- **NFC:** very short-range “tap” communication.
- **Tethering/hotspot:** one device shares its network connection with another.
- **Docking station:** convenient connection point for multiple peripherals and often power/displays/network.
- **Port replicator:** expands available ports; capabilities vary by product.
- **Stylus:** must be compatible; active styluses may require charge/pairing.

When troubleshooting, test with **known-good compatible** cables/accessories and avoid forcing connectors.

## 5. At work
A user wants to arrive at a desk, connect one cable, and immediately use two monitors, Ethernet, keyboard, mouse, and charging. A supported docking station is the right solution.

Another user says a pressure-sensitive stylus “does nothing” while finger touch works. Before replacing the tablet, check stylus compatibility, battery/charge, pairing, and model-specific setup.

## 6. Commands / Tools
- device/dock compatibility matrix
- known-good cable/charger
- Bluetooth settings
- Device Settings / connected-device list
- vendor dock firmware/support page when relevant

## 7. Interview / Explain
**Prompt:** “What is the difference between Bluetooth, NFC, and a hotspot?”

A strong answer distinguishes peripheral/short-range pairing, very-close tap communication, and sharing network access.
