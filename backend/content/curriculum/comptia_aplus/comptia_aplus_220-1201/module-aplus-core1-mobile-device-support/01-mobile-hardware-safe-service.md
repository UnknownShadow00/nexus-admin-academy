# Lesson 1 — Mobile/Laptop Hardware & Safe Service

## 1. What is this?
Mobile support includes more than phones. In A+, the mobile-device hardware scope also covers serviceable **laptop components** such as the battery, keyboard/keys, RAM, HDD/SSD, wireless card, Wi-Fi antennas, camera/webcam, microphone, and physical privacy/security components such as biometrics and near-field scanner features.

Portable devices pack parts tightly together. A repair that would be simple in a desktop can require model-specific disassembly, ribbon cables, antenna routing, adhesives, or vendor procedures.

## 2. Why does an IT worker care?
A junior technician is often expected to **identify the likely component, collect evidence, check the approved procedure, and know when to stop**. Good support does not mean opening every device.

Battery work deserves extra caution. If a battery or device appears damaged, swollen, unusually hot, punctured, or affected by liquid, treat it as a safety issue rather than a normal “keep troubleshooting” problem.

## 4. What you actually need to remember
Before replacing hardware:
- identify the exact device/model and affected component
- confirm symptoms and recent work
- check the approved service manual/SOP
- verify replacement compatibility
- use ESD and power-safety practices appropriate to the device
- preserve screws, cables, antenna routing, seals, and evidence
- make one controlled change at a time

Common clues:
- **weak Wi-Fi after display service:** inspect antenna connectors/routing
- **microphone/camera failure after bezel work:** inspect the nearby connection/placement
- **battery no longer holds charge:** confirm battery health and approved replacement
- **swelling/heat/liquid damage:** stop normal use/charging and follow the safety/escalation process

Do **not** puncture, compress, bend, or “test” a damaged lithium-ion battery by continuing to charge it.

## 5. At work
A laptop returns from a screen repair with extremely weak Wi-Fi. Other devices work normally in the same room. Instead of replacing the wireless card immediately, the technician checks the service history and finds that the Wi-Fi antenna leads run through the display assembly. Inspecting that area is the higher-value next step.

## 6. Commands / Tools
Useful tools vary by vendor:
- device model/serial lookup
- vendor service manual
- asset-management record
- ESD-safe work area
- built-in battery/diagnostic information
- known-good compatible part when authorized

No destructive command is required for this lesson.

## 7. Interview / Explain
**Prompt:** “A laptop has weak Wi-Fi immediately after a display repair. What would you check first and why?”

A strong answer connects the timing of the repair to antenna routing/connectors, uses the service guide, and avoids unrelated part swapping.
