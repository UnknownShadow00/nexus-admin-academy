# 1. What is this?
A desktop PSU converts AC input into DC rails used by components. A+ expects awareness of 110–120 vs 220–240 VAC input, 3.3/5/12V rails, 20+4-pin motherboard power, modular/redundant designs, wattage, and efficiency ratings.

# 2. Why does an IT worker care?
No-power and random-shutdown tickets often involve the power path. Replacement PSUs must fit the system electrically and physically, provide the needed connectors/capacity, and be installed safely.

# 3. Watch / Read
**Required:** Professor Messer — *Computer Power (220-1201)*.

# 4. What you actually need to remember
- Start outside the case: outlet/power strip/UPS, cable, PSU switch/input compatibility, then internal connections.
- The 20+4-pin connector commonly powers the motherboard; CPU/GPU/storage may have separate power connections.
- Higher wattage is not automatically "better"; select adequate capacity/connector/form factor and appropriate efficiency/reliability.
- Modular means detachable PSU cables; **never assume modular cables are interchangeable between PSU models**.
- Never open a PSU for field repair. Stored electrical energy can be dangerous even when unplugged.

# 5. At work
A replacement PSU has enough wattage but lacks the correct GPU power connector. It is not compatible just because the wattage number is large enough.

# 6. Commands / Tools
Use approved outlet/UPS indicators, PSU/system diagnostics, visual connector inspection, and known-good compatible PSU swaps. Electrical measurement requires training and organizational procedure.

# 7. Interview / Explain
Explain your safe first checks for a desktop that shows no lights or fans.
