# 1. What is this?
Technicians work with twisted-pair copper, coax, fiber, USB/USB-C, Thunderbolt, serial, HDMI, DisplayPort, DVI, VGA, SATA/eSATA, and adapters. A+ also expects connector recognition such as RJ11/RJ45/F-type, SC/ST/LC, USB variants, Molex, Lightning, DB-9, and punch-down blocks.

# 2. Why does an IT worker care?
Wrong cable assumptions create dead displays, unstable links, failed storage, damaged ports, and wasted replacement orders. The job is to identify **connector + signal capability + environment**, not just "it fits."

# 3. Watch / Read
**Required:** Professor Messer — *Network Cables*, *Peripheral Cables*, *Video Cables*, and *Copper Connectors*.  
**Optional:** *Optical Fiber*, *Storage Cables*, *Adapters and Converters*, *Fiber Connectors*.

# 4. What you actually need to remember
- Twisted pair may be UTP/STP; environment matters (for example plenum or direct-burial ratings where required).
- T568A/T568B are wiring standards; consistency at both ends matters for ordinary straight-through runs.
- Single-mode and multimode fiber serve different optical/distance designs; SC/ST/LC are common connector families.
- USB-C describes a connector shape; supported data/video/power capabilities can vary by device/cable.
- HDMI/DisplayPort/DVI/VGA are video families; adapters can be passive or require active conversion depending on the signals.
- SATA/eSATA are storage interfaces; Molex is a legacy/common power connector family.
- Never force a connector; verify orientation, cable rating, device support, and adapter direction.

# 5. At work
A USB-C dock powers a laptop but the external monitor stays dark. "USB-C is USB-C" is not enough—verify that the laptop port, dock, and cable support the required display mode.

# 6. Commands / Tools
Visual inspection, manufacturer documentation, cable labeling, known-good cable/adapter, and Module 7 cable-testing tools where applicable.

# 7. Interview / Explain
Explain why the same USB-C connector can support different capabilities on different devices.
