# 1. What is this?
A display is not just "a monitor." A+ expects recognition of LCD panel types such as IPS/TN/VA, OLED and Mini-LED concepts, touchscreens/digitizers, and practical specifications such as resolution, refresh rate, pixel density, and color gamut.

# 2. Why does an IT worker care?
Technicians replace monitors, diagnose "blurry" or "flickering" reports, support conference rooms/projectors, and determine whether a user's complaint is a display setting, cable/input problem, graphics problem, or failed panel.

# 3. Watch / Read
**Required:** Professor Messer — *Display Types* and *Display Attributes (220-1201)*.

# 4. What you actually need to remember
- **IPS:** commonly strong viewing angles/color; **TN:** often fast/low cost; **VA:** often strong contrast. Real products vary.
- **OLED:** pixels emit their own light; can provide excellent contrast but may be susceptible to image retention/burn-in depending on use.
- **Mini-LED:** uses many small LEDs for LCD backlighting/local dimming; it is not the same as OLED.
- **Digitizer:** converts touch/stylus input into coordinates.
- **Resolution:** pixel dimensions; **pixel density:** pixels packed into a physical area; **refresh rate:** how often the image updates; **gamut:** range of reproducible colors.
- A supported/native resolution and appropriate scaling usually produce the clearest desktop image.

# 5. At work
A user says a new 4K monitor makes text "tiny." Replacing the monitor is not the first move. Verify resolution and display scaling, then confirm the user can work comfortably without making the image blurry.

# 6. Commands / Tools
Use OS display settings, the monitor's on-screen menu/self-test, and a known-good cable/device comparison.

# 7. Interview / Explain
Explain resolution vs refresh rate and why they solve different user complaints.
