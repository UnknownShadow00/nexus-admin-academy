# Lesson 3 — Assets, CMDBs, SOPs, Knowledge Bases & SLAs

**Importance:** Working Knowledge  
**Relationship:** New  
**Objectives:** 220-1202 4.1

## 1. What is this?
Support work depends on reliable records.

- **Inventory:** what hardware/software exists.
- **Asset tag/ID:** unique identifier tied to a device record.
- **CMDB (Configuration Management Database):** records configuration items and, in mature environments, relationships/context between them.
- **SOP:** approved repeatable procedure.
- **Knowledge base:** searchable troubleshooting/how-to information.
- **SLA:** agreed service expectations/targets.
- **Onboarding/offboarding checklist:** repeatable steps for employees joining or leaving.

## 2. Why does an IT worker care?
Before replacing a headset, you may need its warranty status. Before changing access, you may need an SOP. Before promising a response time, you need to know the service target.

## 4. What you actually need to remember
Use the organization's system of record instead of guessing.

Common records:
- inventory and assigned user
- warranty/license status
- procurement/lifecycle information
- SOPs
- incident reports
- knowledge articles
- onboarding/offboarding checklists
- SLA targets

Do not memorize enterprise product names. Understand what the record is for and when you would look it up.

## 5. At work
A USB headset develops static. Before ordering a replacement, the technician checks the asset record, confirms the tag/assigned user, and sees that the device is still under warranty.

## 6. Commands / Tools
- Asset-management system
- CMDB
- knowledge base / documentation station
- ticket history

No CLI command is required.

## 7. Interview / Explain example
**Question:** "What's the difference between an inventory list and a CMDB?"  
A useful junior-level answer: inventory mainly tells you what assets exist; a CMDB can also record configuration/context and relationships that help understand services and dependencies.
