# Lesson 4 — Escalation, Handoffs & SLA Awareness

**Importance:** Job Critical  
**Relationship:** Deep Dive  
**Objectives:** 220-1202 4.1, 4.7

## 1. What is this?
Escalation is not failure. It is the correct action when the work needs more skill, authority, access, approval, or urgency than you can safely provide.

A handoff transfers responsibility without losing the investigation.

## 2. Why does an IT worker care?
Junior technicians damage systems when they keep experimenting after reaching the edge of their role. Strong technicians recognize the boundary and send a complete escalation package.

## 4. What you actually need to remember
Escalate when:
- you lack required permissions/approval
- a security/safety concern appears
- the issue is outside your technical scope
- the SLA/impact requires higher-level response
- the approved procedure says to escalate
- troubleshooting reaches a documented stop point

A good handoff includes:
- symptom and business impact
- evidence collected
- actions already taken and results
- what has been ruled out
- current state
- what help/approval is needed
- user expectations or promised update time

## 5. At work
A new employee needs calendar access immediately. The team lead has not yet approved the required group membership. The correct action is **not** to grant access "just this once." Confirm the request, document the blocker, contact the approval path, and update the user.

## 6. Commands / Tools
- ticket escalation fields
- company chat / paging
- knowledge base escalation contacts
- SLA timer/priority indicators

## 7. Interview / Explain example
**Question:** "When should you escalate a ticket?"  
A strong answer emphasizes safety, access/authority, scope, policy, impact/SLA, and sending evidence so Tier 2 does not repeat the investigation.
