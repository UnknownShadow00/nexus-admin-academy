# Lesson 1 — Ticket Intake, Triage & Ownership

**Importance:** Job Critical  
**Relationship:** New / Review  
**Objectives:** 220-1202 4.1, targeted 4.7 clarification practice

## 1. What is this?
A support ticket is the working record for an incident or request. Before troubleshooting, a technician needs enough information to understand **who is affected, what device or service is involved, what the user is experiencing, and how much the issue is affecting work**.

Good intake also means listening before assuming. Users often report symptoms in broad language such as “the internet is broken” or “my computer is slow.” Clarify the statement with an open-ended question before deciding what the problem is.

Triage means deciding what deserves attention first. Do not confuse the loudest user with the highest-impact problem.

## 2. Why does an IT worker care?
Help-desk technicians often have several tickets open at once. A weak intake creates wasted work. A bad priority decision can leave a department outage waiting while a low-impact request gets handled first.

Take ownership of the ticket: know its current state, next action, and next communication point.

## 4. What you actually need to remember
Collect and confirm:
- user/contact information
- affected device/service
- the user's exact symptom in plain language
- impact and urgency
- category/severity according to company rules
- what has already been tried

When the report is vague:
- let the user finish
- ask an open-ended question
- restate the problem to confirm understanding
- do not make a change until you have enough scope

Priority is usually based on **impact + urgency + business rules/SLA**, not personal status or emotion.

## 5. At work
Three tickets arrive:
- warehouse scanners are disconnecting and orders are being recorded on paper
- one designer's PDF editor crashes during a large export
- a new employee cannot see a shared calendar but can continue orientation

The warehouse issue should normally be handled first because the business impact and time pressure are higher.

A fourth user says, “The internet is broken again.” Instead of immediately resetting the adapter, ask the user to walk you through what they were doing and exactly what fails.

## 6. Commands / Tools
No command is required for ticket intake. Useful tools:
- ticketing system
- asset lookup
- knowledge base
- monitoring/status page
- company chat/phone

## 7. Interview / Explain example
**Question:** “You have five tickets open. How do you decide what to work first?”  
A strong answer mentions business impact, urgency, SLA/priority rules, safety/security, and keeping users updated.
